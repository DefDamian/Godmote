# Godmote Support

Need help? Email [dca.support@icloud.com](mailto:dca.support@icloud.com). Please include your device model, iOS version and the Godmote version shown at the bottom of Settings.

## Common questions

**How do I play?**
Pick a power from the bar at the bottom, then touch the world. The small tabs above the bar sort the powers: Pinned (your favourites), Care, Mischief and Disaster. Touch and hold any power to pin or unpin it. Hand flings Motekin, Zap calls lightning, Rain grows berry bushes, Home builds a house and Bless heals. The round buttons drop eggs, switch day and night, turn gravity off, change world and change the weather; the game-controller button opens Play, which has three tabs: Goals (the story, the weekly challenge and challenges), Your world (ages, the monument and the rival tribe) and Arcade. Tap the checklist at the top to see your whims, including one picked out for today.

**My Motekin are hungry, or one has died.**
Motekin eat berries, fish, crops and cooked food. Rain grows bushes, a sea or river lets them fish, and the Fire power gives them somewhere to cook. Use Bless on a Motekin to heal it, or on a fresh memorial to bring someone back. If you would rather nobody starved or grew old, turn off **Motekin need food** and **Motekin grow old** in Settings.

**Things keep happening by themselves.**
Eruptions, weather, seasons, visitors, disasters, the ages, building, starting families, choosing chiefs and villages running themselves (**Let them be**) each have a switch in Settings under **Happens by itself**. With building, families or chiefs switched off, the Motekin wait for you: nothing new is planned, no eggs are laid and no chief is chosen. Disasters, the geyser and the volcano are off until you turn them on.

**My world blew up.**
Meteors that dig to the core, a flood held too long, too much freezing or too many eruptions can end a world. It rebuilds itself after a moment. To stop this happening, turn off **The world can be destroyed** in Settings.

**How do I rename a Motekin, or keep one safe?**
Tap it with the Hand to open its card. The pencil renames it. The star makes it a favourite: favourites survive disasters and drift back if they are flung away. You can have three at a time. The family button shows its family tree.

**How do I give a Motekin a job?**
Open its card and tap the briefcase. Choose Fisher, Cook, Farmer, Builder, Hunter or Guard, or **Let it choose** to leave it alone. With **Let them be** on, a grown Motekin with no work picks whatever its village is short of; you can change it from its card. Fishers and hunters bring food back for the others, cooks cook what is lying about if there is a fire, and a guard stays near home, keeps its neighbours calm and keeps Chompers off them. Babies and the rival tribe cannot be given jobs.

**What do the crops and recipes do?**
Farms grow carrots, corn and pumpkins in turn. When a Motekin cooks a catch over a fire while a farm is ripe, it takes the crop and makes a proper dish. Carrot dishes heal and cure a cold, corn dishes give extra energy for a couple of minutes, and pumpkin dishes keep a Motekin full and happy for a long time. A message tells you each time your cooks work out a new recipe; there are six.

**How do I get a boat?**
The Motekin build a lighthouse beside a sea once the camp has grown into a village. A boat then appears and ferries one Motekin at a time across that sea, sometimes bringing a fish back. It stays in port at night.

**What is under the cave?**
Tap a cave with the Hand to look inside, then tap **Dig down**. Tap the ore to mine it: the builders use it to get a head start on whatever they are building, and each vein grows back after a few minutes. Something large is asleep down there. Every bit of noise brings it closer to waking, and if it wakes the whole world shakes.

**What comes out at night?**
With **Visitors and events** on, fireflies, an owl and a shy ghost turn up after dark and leave at dawn. They are harmless. The owl sits on a tree or building, and the ghost fades if your finger comes near, so tap it quickly.

**What is different about Dune Mote and Cloud Mote?**
Dune Mote is a desert with an oasis. Instead of rain it gets sandstorms, which send everyone home until they pass. On Cloud Mote everything floats: Motekin drift up off the ground by themselves, nothing falls fast, and nobody can be flung away into space.

**What are friends, rivals and the chief?**
Motekin who keep meeting become friends, or now and then fall out. Friends seek each other out and rivals keep apart; the card shows who is who. Once four or more adults share a home or a building, they choose a chief, who wears a red sash and has a say in what gets built next. If the chief is lost they choose again. Under **Remembers**, each card lists the three biggest things that have happened to that Motekin.

**What is a cold, and how do I cure it?**
Colds only happen if you turn on **Motekin can catch colds** in Settings; it is off to begin with. A cold makes a Motekin slow, gives it a red nose and can pass to anyone standing close. It wears off by itself after a couple of minutes and never kills. Bless cures it at once, and the village will plant a herb garden where the sick can go to get better.

**How do I shape my own world?**
Open the globe button and visit **My Mote**. A Shape tab appears with Sea, River, Hill, Cave, Rub out and Paint. Tap the ground to place one.

**How do I look inside the sea or a cave?**
With the Hand, tap a cave to look inside, or tap the sea and then **Look under the sea**.

**What are challenges and the arcade?**
The game-controller button opens both. A challenge borrows a fresh world for anything from under a minute to five minutes; your own world is saved first and comes back untouched. The arcade has five small games; beating a target wins an outfit you can put on any Motekin.

**What is the story?**
Ten short chapters played on your own world. Each gives you one thing to do, and the last asks you to choose between being a kind god or a cruel one. Nothing starts by itself: open the game-controller button and tap Begin the story. Tap Hide on the chapter card to put it away, and carry on later from the same menu. Once it is told you can tell it again.

**What is the weekly challenge?**
One bigger goal each week, such as feeding your Motekin forty times. It counts what happens on your own worlds, and a new goal arrives every Monday. Finishing it wins an outfit that can only be won that way. Progress is under the game-controller button.

**How do I raise a statue?**
When your Motekin love you they leave offerings at a shrine; when they fear you they leave tribute at a barricade. Tap one to take it. Three saved offerings pay for a statue: open the game-controller button, choose Your world and look under Monument. The Motekin build it themselves. A statue raised in love is golden, one raised in fear is dark, and three more offerings make it grander, twice over.

**What are the ages, and how do I get a rocket?**
A town with at least four grown Motekin slowly gets ready for something new. When it is ready the builders put up a forge, and the Bronze Age begins; later a street lamp starts the Lantern Age, and a launch pad starts the Rocket Age. Builders work faster from the Bronze Age on, and lamplight keeps Motekin cheerful after dark. In the Rocket Age the Motekin build a rocket on the pad. When it is ready, open the game-controller button, choose Your world, look under Age and pick a world, or the moon: up to three grown Motekin fly there and are waiting when you visit. The chief and at least one other always stay behind, and favourites are the last to be picked. Then they start on another rocket. If the world ends, the town starts again in the Stone Age. To keep a town as it is, turn off **Towns move through the ages** in Settings.

**What is the Moon Base?**
The first rocket you send to the moon founds a base there. After that, **Moon Base** appears in the worlds list, free, and a light shows on the moon in the sky. Nobody is born there to start with: it has one home, the ship that landed, and whoever flew in, and it grows from there. Gravity is low. You can send more Motekin by rocket or from their cards.

**What is the chronicle?**
Each world keeps a record of what happens on it: births, friendships and feuds, new buildings, new ages, launches, festivals and disasters. Open the game-controller button, choose Your world and look under Chronicle. **Read the whole chronicle** shows every day, and the share button sends it as text. It holds the last 80 lines.

**What are the festivals?**
Five times a year, for a few days, the Motekin hang flags on their homes and dance: Starfall over the new year, Bloom Days in March, The Long Day in June, Lantern Nights at the end of October and Gift Days in late December. The first one you see wins a festival rosette they can wear. Festivals follow the date on your device. To go without them, turn off **Seasonal festivals** in Settings.

**My town is getting crowded. Can I make the planet bigger?**
Yes. Settings, then **Planet size**: Small, Normal, Large or Huge. A bigger planet makes Motekin and buildings smaller, so there is more room and the town can put up more buildings. A smaller one does the opposite, and on a town that is already full the buildings may overlap. **Zoom**, just below it, only changes how close you look; you can also pinch. The limit on how many Motekin can live on a world does not change: 16, or 32 with Crowded Mote.

**How do I get a second village?**
Let one grow. Once eight or more Motekin live in a village that has built a few things, a few of them (three to five, more from a bigger village) set out to found another on open ground far from the first. Each village has a name, a banner, a belt in its own colour and its own chief. A Normal planet has room for two villages, and Large and Huge for three; a Small planet keeps to one. See them under the game-controller button, **Your world**, **Villages**.

**Why are my villages trading, or raiding each other?**
Villages warm to each other when their people are happy and fed, and cool when they are miserable or hungry, so how you treat them sets the mood. Good cheer alone only goes so far: it takes trade to make allies, and neighbours quarrel now and then over small things, more often when they live off the same thing or are hungry. Friendly villages send traders, and close ones become allies; an alliance can lapse and be made again. The **Villages** list shows the last thing that happened between each pair and how many times they have traded. Villages that fall out send raiders who steal food and cause a scene; a Guard on watch sees them off. Nobody is hurt in a raid. Cheer both villages up and they make peace; now and then one of them sends a gift to mend things by itself.

**Why did a Motekin move to another village?**
When one village has grown much bigger than another, now and then somebody packs up and moves to the smaller one, taking their children along. It keeps small villages from dying out.

**I would rather run things myself.**
Turn off **Let them be** in Settings. No new villages are founded, villages leave each other alone, nobody moves house or picks a job unasked, and no wonder is started or worked on. Villages that already exist stay.

**How do I get a closer look at my world?**
Pinch to zoom in. Keep going past the closest view of the planet and you land on the ground where your fingers were: the world unrolls flat and close, so you can watch a village at work. Drag the sky to look around, pinch to move closer or further, and pinch out or tap **Whole planet** to go back. You can use every power in the ground view. Settings also has a button for it, and a switch, **Pinch in for a ground view**, to turn it off. Worlds that are already flat do not need it.

**How do I find where something happened?**
A message with a small target on it is about a place: tap it and the camera goes there. In the game-controller button, **Your world**, **Villages**, the target beside each village and the wonder does the same. In the ground view, a small flag at the edge of the screen points to each village that is out of sight; tap it to go there. On a round world this lands you in the ground view, so it needs **Pinch in for a ground view** switched on.

**How do I pause or speed things up?**
The play button in the row of round buttons. Tap it once for fast (three times normal), again to pause, and again for normal. Challenges always run at normal speed.

**What are village specialities and roads?**
Each village is known for something, decided by the land it stands on: fishing by water, mining near a cave, otherwise farming or making. A fishing village lands extra fish, a farming village's fields ripen sooner, a mining village turns up ore, and a makers' village finishes buildings faster. Traders carry these goods, and villages with different specialities get more out of trading. After three trades between the same two villages a dotted road appears between them, and traders walk it faster.

**What are commandments?**
Rules you lay down for the whole world: game-controller button, **Your world**, **Commandments**. Pick up to three, such as Share your food, No raids or Work hard. A village follows them if its people adore you, fear you, or have heard your prophet in the last few minutes. A village that isn't sure about you ignores them; the list shows which is which.

**What is a prophet?**
One grown Motekin who speaks for you. Open its card and tap the megaphone; it wears a halo. It walks from village to village, and any village it has just visited follows your commandments. Motekin near it grow more cheerful if they like you, and a crowd around it deepens whatever they already think of you. If you lose your prophet, everyone's spirits drop and the villages cool towards each other. Tap the megaphone again to let it stand down.

**What is the wonder?**
When two villages become allies they start a great tower between them. Once begun it keeps rising even if the alliance lapses, and stops only while the two are feuding. It goes faster the more grown Motekin they have, and takes a good while. Once finished it stays, and everyone on that world is a little happier from then on. One wonder per world.

**Where is the world's history?**
Game-controller button, **Your world**, **World history**: a chart of population over time, the world in numbers, and its turning points taken from the chronicle.

**How do I move a Motekin to another world?**
Tap the Motekin with the Hand, then **Send to another world** on its card. It will be waiting the next time you visit that world.

**How do I take a picture?**
Settings, then **Photo mode**. The buttons hide and everything holds still; use **Save or share**. Godmote can only add the picture you choose to save, and cannot see your photo library.

**How do I make a postcard?**
In photo mode, the **frame** button steps through five frames, **Caption** writes a line across the bottom, and the row of stickers lets you pick one and tap the picture to place it. The arrow at the end of the row removes the last sticker. Tap the picture with no sticker picked to hide the buttons and see the whole thing.

**How do I show a friend my world?**
Settings, then **Invite a friend to visit this world**. It makes a line of text starting with MOTE2 that you can send any way you like. Your friend opens Settings, taps **Use a friend's code** and pastes it in. They get a copy of your world to look round, with its buildings and its Motekin by name. Nothing they do there changes your world or theirs, and they tap **Go home** when they are done. There is no server and no account.

**What is Leave a gift?**
While visiting, tap **Leave a gift** and send the line of text it makes back to your friend. When they paste it under **Use a friend's code**, they get one offering towards a statue. Each gift can be opened once, and three a day at most.

**How do I share just the shape of My Mote?**
Settings, then **Share the shape of My Mote**. That code starts with MOTE1 and holds only the seas, rivers, hills, caves and ground colour. Pasting it reshapes your friend's My Mote; whoever lives there stays.

**Where are my stats?**
Settings, then **Stats**. It adds up every world together: Motekin hatched, meteors dropped, fish caught, worlds ended and more. Counting began when the page was added, the totals stay on your device, and **Reset stats** sets them back to zero.

**How do I save a video?**
Turn on **Replay clips** in Settings and allow recording when iOS asks. A record button appears next to Play. Tap it after something good happens to save the last ten seconds.

**How do I keep my worlds on two devices?**
Turn on **iCloud sync** in Settings on both, signed in to the same Apple Account. For each world, the device that saved last wins, so finish on one before opening the other.

**Is there a widget?**
Yes. Touch and hold the home screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and pick Godmote. It shows your world, who lives there, the age the town has reached and the latest line of its chronicle, and updates each time you leave the game.

**How do I play in Spanish?**
Godmote follows your device language. To change only this game, open the Settings app, then Apps, Godmote, Language.

**A Motekin drifted away and never came back.**
Flung hard enough, a Motekin escapes the Mote's gravity for good. Drop an egg with the plus button to hatch a new one.

**I bought something and it is still locked.**
Open the store in the game and tap **Restore Purchases** while signed in to the Apple Account you bought it with. If a purchase is waiting for Ask to Buy approval, it unlocks as soon as it is approved. Tips unlock nothing, so there is nothing to restore for them.

**How do I get a refund?**
Refunds are handled by Apple at [reportaproblem.apple.com](https://reportaproblem.apple.com).

**There is no sound.**
Godmote follows the silent switch. Check that Silent Mode is off, the volume is up, and Sound effects is on in Settings.

**How do I start my whims over?**
Settings, then **Reset whims**. This does not affect purchases.
